01

The dashboard & your pipelines

Your home screen: what's in the pipeline, what it's worth, and what needs doing.

DealDesk
›

Read the numbers at a glance

The Home page shows four headline tiles — open pipeline (total loan $), weighted pipeline (each deal's loan $ times its stage's close-probability), open deals, and lenders in your book — plus a bar chart of loan $ per stage for each pipeline, and a live activity feed.

Weighted vs. open: "open" is the raw total; "weighted" discounts each deal by how likely its stage is to close, so it's a more honest forecast.
›

Work the boards

Click Deals. Deals live on two Kanban boards — Acquisition and Financing — one column per stage. To move a deal, use the little stage dropdown on its card; the board re-sorts instantly. Click a card to open the deal.

›

Add or import deals

+ New deal opens a quick form. Import CSV bulk-loads deals (name required; the rest optional). Export downloads the current pipeline as CSV — the same format the importer reads, so it round-trips.

02

Scout — your AI deal agent

Ask about your deals, or tell it to do something. Needs your Anthropic key.

DealDesk
Two modes, one box. Scout answers questions and takes actions. With no key it degrades to keyword search over your deals; with a key it becomes a tool-using agent.
›

Ask it anything about your book

From Home, type into the ask box. "Which of my deals are closest to closing?" · "Summarize my financing pipeline." · "Which deals have open tasks?" Scout reads a live snapshot of your deals and answers, citing deal names.

›

Tell it to take an action

Scout can create and update deals, run an underwriting model, match lenders, read a deal's documents, draft email, and add tasks — all from chat.

try:"Create an acquisition deal called Sunset Plaza — office in Denver, $30M, 60% LTV, 6.5% cap — then run the model."
What happens: Scout creates the deal (you'll find it on the board), sizes the loan and NOI, and reports DSCR / debt yield. If something's missing — say, no interest rate — it tells you and asks, rather than inventing a number.
›

Ask about a deal's documents

Upload a term sheet or OM to a deal (see 07), then ask Scout about it: "What rate did the lender quote on Harbor Pointe?" It reads the extracted text and answers from the document.

03

The underwriting-model builder

A lender-grade model from the deal's fields — pure finance math, no key needed.

DealDesk
›

Build the model

Open a deal → Build underwriting model. DealDesk fills the gaps (NOI from price×cap, loan from price×LTV), sizes the debt, and shows DSCR, debt yield, cash-on-cash, and a multi-year pro forma. Tune amortization, NOI growth, hold, exit cap, and interest-only inline — every metric updates live.

›

See the exit & returns

The model projects a sale at the end of the hold (forward NOI ÷ exit cap, less costs and loan payoff) and reports a levered IRR and equity multiple. A rate × cap DSCR sensitivity grid shows how coverage holds up as pricing and values move.

›

Compare loan scenarios

Loan scenarios puts several structures side by side — as-entered, interest-only, a +100 bps rate stress, and an index + spread quote priced off your Base rates (pick a benchmark, set the spread in bps).

›

Download the Excel

download .xlsx saves the whole model — sources & uses, debt sizing, returns, pro forma, and the sensitivity grid — as a real spreadsheet you can hand to a lender.

All deterministic. No Anthropic key is used or needed for any of this — it's finance math you can audit.
04

Lender matching, comps & placements

Find the right capital, see what similar loans priced at, and track who you've shopped.

DealDesk
›

Match lenders

On a deal → Match lenders ranks your own lender book against the deal, with a 0–100 fit score and plain-English reasons. Lenders whose box the deal can't fit are shown separately with why. With a key, AI pros/cons adds a one-line take per lender.

›

Shop it — and track placements

Hit + shop on a matched lender and it becomes a Placement on the deal — where you took it, its status (shopped → quoted → selected), and terms. The Placements panel is the record of who you called.

›

See comparable loans

Comparable loans matches your closed-loan comps to the deal (asset type, state, leverage) and reads out an indicated market rate — the median of similar closed loans — so you know what's fair before you quote it.

It's your data. There's no live market feed — the comps are the ones you seed and maintain, so the pricing signal is only as current as you keep it.
05

Market data & offering memoranda

Your capital-markets reference set, and a lender-ready OM in one click.

DealDesk
›

Keep your Market current

Market holds your lender Directory, Base rates (loan-pricing benchmarks — edit them inline as they move), and Recent terms (closed-loan comps you can filter by asset/state/text or import from CSV).

›

Generate an offering memorandum

On a deal → Offering memo builds a full sectioned OM: executive summary, investment highlights, financing request, a financial summary (pulled from the underwriting model), comparable financing (from your comps), and sponsor. With a key the prose is AI-written; without, it's filled from the deal's own fields.

Download it: download .html saves a standalone, print-ready file — and your browser's Print → Save as PDF turns it into a PDF.
06

Documents & the email loop

Extract terms from a file; keep a real email thread on every deal.

DealDesk
›

Upload & extract terms

On a deal → Upload document (term sheet, OM, email — PDF, .docx, or text). DealDesk pulls out structured terms (loan, LTV, rate, DSCR, cap…). Tick apply to write them straight onto the deal. The original file is kept in the Documents vault to view or download later.

With a key, extraction uses the AI (far better on messy prose — it'll pick the all-in rate over the spread). Without, it uses regex on the common patterns.
›

Sync & thread email

Once your inbox is connected (Setup Guide, Phase 3), sync inbox pulls recent mail and files it onto the right deal (by the sender's contact, or the deal name in the subject). Each deal gets an Emails panel — a real thread of in and out messages.

›

Reply, template, send

Reply on an inbound message pre-fills the recipient and quotes the original. + compose starts fresh, with follow-up templates (Follow-up, Request term sheet, Intro/teaser, Status update) that fill in from the deal. Sending stores the message on the thread.

07

Staying in control

Your data out, your workspace safe, your spend capped, your way.

DealDesk
›

Export & back up

Backup (top nav) exports deals, lenders, contacts, and comps to CSV, and downloads your whole workspace as one SQLite .db file. You can restore from a backup there too — it validates the file and keeps a .bak of the current one first.

›

Adjust your AI spend cap — anytime

The header always shows AI spend $X / $Y — real cost to date over your monthly cap. To change the cap, go to Settings → Monthly AI-spend cap (cents), enter a new number (2500 = $25, 10000 = $100), and Save. It applies immediately.

What the cap does: once your metered spend reaches it, DealDesk refuses paid AI calls (they fall back to the free rules/templates or ask you to raise the cap) until the next calendar month — so a busy day can't run up a surprise bill.
›

Make it yours

Dark mode — the toggle in the header, or press d. Keyboard shortcuts — press ? for the list (g d to Deals, / to focus the ask box, n for a new deal). Reset — Settings → Workspace → "Reload demo data" or "Clear everything" to start fresh (your keys are kept).
